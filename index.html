The research story

From Pickering emulsions at IIT Madras to microalgae in Seoul — a decade of curiosity at fluid interfaces.

2017 – 2024 · IIT Madras

Ph.D. research — engineering emulsions, particle by particle

For seven years at IIT Madras, I dedicated myself to one beautiful question: how can we get tiny solid particles to stabilize oil-and-water mixtures so well that they replace harsh chemical surfactants entirely? The answer became my thesis. By precisely tuning particle wettability, I formulated a family of Pickering emulsions — single, double, multiple, and non-aqueous — that hold together for months. Then I flipped the problem and showed how everyday solutes like ethanol can selectively break them apart on demand.

This work earned me the Institute Research Award in 2023 and three first-author papers in Langmuir and the Journal of Colloid and Interface Science.

Why this matters
Science
Pushed the boundary on how shape and surface chemistry govern emulsion behaviour.
Industry
A foundation for surfactant-free cosmetics, food emulsions, and pharmaceutical carriers.
Society
Cleaner consumer products, less synthetic surfactant runoff in our water systems.
Industrial Collaboration · ISRO

With ISRO — fuelling the next generation of rockets

One of the most thrilling chapters of my Ph.D. was a direct industrial collaboration with the Indian Space Research Organisation. The challenge: design a non-aqueous Pickering emulsion fuel blend stable enough for aerospace use — where the chemistry of standard surfactants simply cannot survive. We worked on getting solid colloidal particles to stabilize the interface between two non-aqueous liquids, opening a new pathway for safer, energy-dense propellant systems.

Working with ISRO scientists meant my lab science had to translate into real-world performance constraints — pressure, temperature, shelf life. It was a masterclass in turning curiosity into capability.

Why this matters
Science
First-of-its-kind solid-stabilized emulsions in non-aqueous propellant systems.
Industry
Contributes to India's indigenous space programme and advanced propulsion R&D.
Society
Stronger national capability in space exploration — satellites for weather, agriculture, disaster response.
Industrial Collaboration · Shell

With Shell — decarbonizing oil products through lignin chemistry

How do you decarbonize an oil company from the inside? You ask better questions about chemistry. In partnership with Shell, I worked on the surface activity of lignin — a natural, abundant polymer that is currently the second-most-waste byproduct of the paper industry — and explored how it can substitute for fossil-based surfactants in oil product formulations.

This is the kind of science that quietly reshapes a trillion-dollar industry: take an under-used bio-resource, understand its interfacial behaviour at the molecular level, and turn it into a sustainable replacement for petrochemical additives.

Why this matters
Science
Decoded lignin's surface activity for high-performance, bio-based formulations.
Industry
A direct contribution to the energy sector's net-zero and circular-economy goals.
Society
Less reliance on petro-surfactants; turning waste lignin into value, less carbon in the atmosphere.
June 2025 – Present · Kyung Hee University

Postdoctoral work — microalgae at fluid interfaces

I'm now in Seoul, exploring a question at the crossroads of soft matter and biology: how do living microalgae behave at fluid interfaces, and what controls their interactions there? Microalgae are tiny photosynthetic cells that may quietly become some of the most important organisms of the 21st century — they can produce biofuel, capture atmospheric CO₂, feed populations, and clean wastewater. To harness them, we need to understand how they assemble and flow at interfaces.

This is the most exciting chapter yet — bringing the rigour of colloid science to a living system with enormous sustainability potential.

Why this matters
Science
Bridges classical colloid science and microbial biophysics — a frontier with very few rules written yet.
Industry
Foundational for next-gen bioreactors, biofuel harvesting, and algae-based food/feed processing.
Society
Carbon capture, sustainable nutrition, cleaner water, and a viable path away from fossil-based fuels.
Ongoing · Active matter

Active Janus particles — colloids that swim

Alongside my postdoctoral work, I continue exploring the dynamics of active Janus particles — asymmetric colloids that propel themselves using local chemical or thermal gradients. They behave less like passive grains and more like tiny living organisms, and they sit at the heart of one of physics' most vibrant frontiers: active soft matter.

The potential downstream applications are extraordinary: targeted drug delivery inside the human body, environmental clean-up at the microscale, programmable lab-on-chip diagnostics.

Why this matters
Science
Active matter is rewriting how we understand non-equilibrium systems and self-organization.
Industry
Underpins next-gen microrobotics, micro-fluidic diagnostics, and precision drug delivery.
Society
Imagine medicine delivered directly to a tumour, or pollutants cleaned up by armies of microswimmers.
